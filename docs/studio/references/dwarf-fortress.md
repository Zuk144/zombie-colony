# Dwarf Fortress (Bay 12 Games, 2006–; Steam edition 2022)

**Why it's here (owner):** the ancestor of the whole genre, and RimWorld's own inspiration.
It's the deepest story generator ever made.

**Genre:** a colony simulation with indirect control. It generates a whole world with centuries
of history, then you guide seven dwarves to build a fortress. You designate (dig here, build
that, make this) and dwarves choose what to do. Its community motto is **"Losing is fun."**

## How it plays

You pick a site from a generated world with real history (civilizations, wars, named
megabeasts, artifacts). You dig into a mountain, set up farms, workshops, and stockpiles, and
the fortress grows through migrant waves. Nobles arrive with demands, and sieges, caverns,
forgotten beasts, and floods threaten it. There's **no win condition.** Every fortress
eventually falls, and the fall is the story.

## Systems that make stories

- **Stress and memory** (the model behind RimWorld's mood). Emotions add stress points. There's
  short-term stress (the visible mood) and long-term stress built from memories that got
  "promoted" and resurface. Thresholds are stressed → haggard → harrowed → insane. A breakdown
  needs *both* short- and long-term stress to be high. Personality facets (bravery, stress
  vulnerability, anxiety) set how fast each dwarf breaks and recovers. **Recovery takes years;
  prevention beats cure.**
- **What prevents it:** a nice bedroom (engraved 3×3: 99% of dwarves feel bliss), a legendary
  dining room, meaningful work that builds valued skills, and military training (93% form happy
  memories). Death, corpses, miasma, and trauma drive stress up.
- **The tantrum spiral:** one unhappy dwarf tantrums and hurts someone, others grieve, more
  tantrum, and the fortress eats itself. This is the classic DF collapse, a cascade you can see
  coming.
- **Strange moods:** past 20 dwarves, a dwarf may be seized by inspiration, claim a workshop,
  and demand specific materials. Supply them and you get a **legendary artifact** plus a master
  crafter. Fail within about 42 days and they go insane. Dark variants (fell, macabre) need
  bones, or a murder. Your economy's gaps become a person's tragedy.
- **Everything is remembered:** artifacts get names and histories, and **Legends mode** lets you
  read your fortress's story after it falls. Later players can reclaim ruined forts or visit
  them in adventure mode.
- **Emergent physics:** fluids (water, magma) flow and flood, and temperature, materials, and
  wounds are simulated down to tissue layers. Some of the most famous stories are simulation
  surprises (drunken cats, magma floods).

## Art and UX

- Originally ASCII (letters as creatures). The Steam edition added pixel tiles and a mouse UI,
  and that opened the game to a far larger audience. **Accessibility was the bottleneck, not
  depth.**
- Historically the least approachable UI in gaming: nested menus, codes, a lot of wiki time.

## Why it's fun

1. **Stories nobody wrote.** Players post fortress sagas (the famous "Boatmurdered" succession
   game) because the systems produce drama.
2. **Failure as content.** Losing isn't the end of the fun; the collapse *is* the climax.
3. **Everyone is a person** with preferences ("likes rose gold and cats"), grudges, friends, and
   memories.
4. **Depth rewards curiosity.** There's always a system you didn't know about.

## What to take (principles)

- **Losing is fun: design the collapse to be a story.** Our colony falling should be a legend
  worth telling, with a readable cascade (breach → bite → turning → grief), not a sudden game
  over.
- **Prevention through place.** Good bedrooms, dining, and meaningful work keep people sane; we
  have rooms and thoughts, so lean on them.
- **Named things and memory.** Survivors remember who died and who saved them. A "Legends"-lite
  colony chronicle at the end of a run would be a strong feature (a Phase 5 candidate).
- **Moods that tie economy to people:** "Lou won't stop talking about the old radio; bring her
  components." Missing materials become a personal drama, not a number.
- **Reclaiming a fallen colony** as a new start fits a zombie world perfectly: your old survivors
  are out there, wearing their old clothes.

## What to avoid

- **Opaque UI and wiki-dependence.** DF became mainstream only once it became readable. We're
  touch-first; nothing important can hide.
- **Simulation depth nobody sees.** Tissue layers and fluid physics are only worth it where they
  surface as stories or decisions.
- **No goal at all.** DF can do it; a tablet game needs arcs (our Phase 3.5 → radio tower gives
  one).

## How it maps to us

We're DF's lineage via RimWorld, with DF's best idea to reclaim: **the collapse is the story.**
Our twist is that the dead come back. A fallen colony isn't gone; it's a place full of your old
people, and a future colony could reclaim it.
