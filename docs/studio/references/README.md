## What the owner's list says about their taste

Every game here is a **systemic builder with a threat, played for long sessions, where stories
emerge from rules.** None are narrative-scripted. Most are top-down and readable. Several are
one- or two-person studios (Banished, Songs of Syx, Manor Lords, early DF), so depth doesn't need
a big team.

## Principles that recur (use these as the studio's shared vocabulary)

1. **Your growth causes your threat.** TAB (land outside the walls), Factorio (pollution buys
   biters), RimWorld (wealth draws raids), Zomboid (noise). **Our version is the Din,** and it's the
   most readable of all because you can hear it and turn it down.
2. **A small leak cascades.** TAB (one infected building), DF (tantrum spirals), Zomboid (one
   bite). A cascade should be *visible early* and *stoppable*, so it feels like your fault, not
   the game's.
3. **Failure is the story.** DF, RimWorld, Zomboid, Cosmoteer. Design the collapse to be worth
   retelling; never delete a run without a narrative.
4. **Visible logistics, fixed by layout.** Cosmoteer (crew carry batteries), Manor Lords (the ox
   drags logs), Banished, Prison Architect. **This is our "no belts" answer:** people carrying
   things is the economy, and you fix it by moving things, not by giving orders.
5. **Renewal vs depletion.** Banished (the forester replants; mines run out), Zomboid (loot doesn't
   respawn), Factorio (ore patches drain and push you outward). **Renewables keep you alive;
   depletion pushes you outward.** That's our resource loop (DESIGN §8).
6. **Variety drives the upgrade ladder.** Manor Lords (2 then 3 food types), Songs of Syx (race
   preferences), DF (legendary dining rooms). "More different stuff" beats "more stuff," and it's a
   reason to reclaim different districts.
7. **Policies, not orders.** Prison Architect (the regime), Songs of Syx (decrees), Norland
   (govern through nobles), RimWorld (priorities). Every lever has a visible cost: lockdown causes
   cabin fever, overtime causes misery.
8. **Scheduled dread.** TAB (a swarm announced 8 hours ahead with a direction and music), Factorio
   (evolution). Tension works when the player can see it coming and prepare.
9. **People are the point.** DF, RimWorld, Norland, Banished's families. Names, memories, and
   relationships are what make a system into a story.
10. **Readable at a glance.** Prison Architect, Cosmoteer, and Songs of Syx at scale: flat shapes,
    clear icons, status balloons, and stats shown *while you place things.*

## The art spectrum

The references split into **clean and diagrammatic** (Prison Architect, Cosmoteer, Songs of Syx,
RimWorld) and **richly rendered** (Manor Lords, They Are Billions, Zomboid). We're firmly on the
clean side (vector, touch, any zoom). The rendered games teach *mood*: seasons, light, a swarm as
a creeping mass. We get mood from **light, color, weather, and motion,** not texture.
