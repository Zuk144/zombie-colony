# Manor Lords (Slavic Magic / Hooded Horse; early access April 2024)

**Why it's here (owner):** the most beautiful organic city builder, where a town grows the way
real medieval towns did.

**Genre:** a medieval city builder with real-time tactical battles, made mostly by one
developer and inspired by 14th-century Franconia. It favors historical authenticity over fantasy
tropes. It was one of the biggest early-access launches ever.

## How it plays

You start with a handful of families, an ox, and a cart. You lay out roads freely (no grid),
draw **burgage plots** (house lots) along them, and the village grows outward from a central
marketplace. The seasons drive farming. Bandits and rival lords push you into militia and
battles, and you expand by claiming regions, each specializing in different resources.

## Systems worth knowing

- **Burgage plots (freeform housing):** you draw a lot of any shape along a road for 2 timber.
  Its frontage decides how many houses fit, and its depth decides whether it gets a backyard. Each
  living space holds one family (3 people). Each plot needs 1 fuel a month, which a resident
  fetches from the market.
- **Backyard extensions:** tier 1 (vegetable garden, chicken coop, goat or pig pen, orchard,
  apiary) keeps the family available for other jobs, some producing passively. Tier 2 (blacksmith,
  brewery, tailor, bowyer, butcher) **turns the family into artisans permanently.** It's a big,
  irreversible choice: self-sufficiency vs labor pool.
- **Levels through variety:** a plot upgrades when its needs are met. Level 1 needs basics; level 2
  needs 2 food types, fuel, clothing, and church access (4 timber plus planks); level 3 needs 3
  food types, a tavern with ale, a stone church, and footwear (plus 25 regional wealth, planks, and
  roof tiles). Higher levels **pay regional wealth** (1–2 per family per month) and let militia
  wear better armor. **The town's prosperity and its army's quality are the same ladder.**
- **Approval (0–100%)** has seven stages. Start at 50% (Respected, neutral). Liked (55–79%) brings
  +1 family a month; Revered (95%+) brings +3 a month, passive income, and militia morale. Reviled
  (below 5%) means no taxes, people leaving, and no militia. It combines *recent factors* with
  *lingering memories* at half strength that fade over 90 days. **People remember.**
- **Logistics you can see:** an ox drags every log, carts carry goods, and families walk to the
  market stalls to take what they need. Distance and roads visibly matter.
- **Environmental cost:** overhunting drives deer away, overcutting deforests, and fields lose
  fertility per crop.
- **Two treasuries:** *regional wealth* (the people's, earned by plots) vs your personal treasury
  (taxes and tithes). Taxing hurts approval.
- **War takes workers:** raising militia pauses those families' jobs, while mercenaries cost
  treasury. Battles use fatigue, formations, and weather.
- **Development points** per region: a perk tree (e.g. better food-variety approval, better
  smiths) makes each region specialize.

## Art and UX

- **Stunning, grounded realism:** muddy roads that form where people walk, timber-framed houses,
  seasons, and weather. The camera can drop to street level and walk through your town.
- Organic layout is the visual signature. No two towns look the same because **roads follow
  terrain and people.**
- The UI is fairly clean for the genre: a top bar (approval as a thumbs-up), building panels, and
  overlays for fertility and resources.

## Why it's fun

1. **Beauty from function:** the town looks right *because* it works. Organic growth is
   satisfying to watch.
2. **A clear, visible ladder:** feed variety → approval → families → wealth → better homes →
   better soldiers. You can see each rung.
3. **Irreversible commitments** (artisan backyards, regions) make choices feel weighty.
4. **Walk your town:** being able to stand in the street makes it personal.

## What to take (principles)

- **Freeform drawing on touch:** burgage plots are drawn, not stamped. Our fence and zone tools
  should feel this fluid: drag a line, and it follows your finger.
- **Variety drives the upgrade ladder.** It turns "more stuff" into "different stuff," which is a
  reason to scavenge widely and reclaim different districts (pharmacy, hardware, and so on).
- **Lingering memories that fade** (at half effect, over 90 days): a clean, readable model for
  colony morale after a tragedy.
- **An irreversible specialization choice.** For us, maybe a survivor who commits to being the
  colony's mechanic or doctor for life.
- **Prosperity and defense on the same ladder:** better homes mean better-armed militia. For us,
  a thriving colony should be a stronger one, not only a bigger target.
- **Visible logistics:** people carrying things *is* the economy. We already do this with
  haulers; keep it on screen.

## What to avoid

- **Realistic 3D art and a street-level camera.** Wrong medium for us; we're clean top-down vector.
- **Real-time tactical battles with direct command.** We're indirect.
- **Two treasuries and taxes.** Too much economy-sim for a survival camp.

## How it maps to us

Manor Lords shows how to make growth *readable and beautiful*, with a town that tells you what it
needs through its shape. For us, the lesson is the upgrade ladder driven by **variety,** and the
feel of **drawing** your settlement with a finger. Imagine the secure yard growing district by
district as organically as a Manor Lords town, with the fence line following the terrain and the
streets you reclaimed.
