# Banished (Shining Rock Software, 2014)

**Why it's here (owner):** the pure survival city builder. There are no enemies, and nature and
arithmetic are the threat.

**Genre:** a survival city builder made by one developer. A group of exiled families starts
over in a valley with a cart of supplies. There's no combat and no money at first; **the only
currency is people,** and the only enemies are winter, hunger, disease, and bad luck.

## How it plays

You place homes, woodcutters, gatherers, hunters, and fields, and assign jobs by number (not
by person). Citizens live their own lives: they walk home to eat, grab firewood, marry, have
children, age, and die. The first winter is the famous wall. Later you manage generations:
schools, tailors, blacksmiths, trade, and nomads who ask to join.

## Systems worth knowing

- **Population is the resource, and it's generational.** New homes let couples move in and have
  children, and children take years to become workers. A "baby boom" becomes a wave of workers
  15 years later, then a wave of old age. Overbuilding homes at once creates an **age cliff**
  decades later.
- **Citizens aren't mindless:** they go home when hungry, warm up at a neighbor's house in the
  cold, idle where they're happy when depressed, and stock their own homes with food and fuel.
- **Warmth:** homes need firewood (stone houses use less), and **clothing lets people walk
  farther in winter.** Distance from home to work is a survival variable.
- **Health through diet variety:** grain, fruit, vegetables, protein. Herbs make up the shortfall,
  and hospitals contain disease.
- **Tools wear out.** Workers without tools are slow, so a blacksmith turns iron plus wood into a
  steady tool supply, one of the game's quiet dependency chains.
- **Education:** about +30% productivity. Standing jobs (smith, fisher) benefit more than walking
  jobs.
- **Renewables vs deposits:** **foresters plant and cut trees** (a renewable forest), and orchards,
  pastures, fishing, and hunting all renew. Mines and quarries eventually run out, and the map's
  stone and iron are finite unless you trade.
- **Disasters:** starvation, early frost killing crops, freezing, pest infestations that spread
  between same-crop fields (so diversify), sickness after trade or nomads, tornadoes, and fires
  that citizens fight by carrying water from wells or rivers.
- **Status icons over heads:** homeless, starving, freezing, no workplace, no tool, sick. The
  whole health of the town is readable from icons.

## Art and UX

- Calm, naturalistic 3D with seasons sweeping across the valley: snow, bare trees, green
  springs. The landscape *is* the calendar.
- Minimal UI: job counts, stock numbers, a few overlays. You watch people more than panels.
- Quiet, melancholic music. It feels like hard, humble work.

## Why it's fun

1. **Planning against time.** Winter is coming, and you can see how much firewood you have.
2. **Consequences echo for decades.** Decisions made in year 2 show up in year 30 as an aging
   population or a shortage of adults.
3. **Humble stakes that feel huge.** One family freezing because you miscounted logs hits hard.
4. **Renewal loops:** the forester replants, the orchard regrows, and the town learns to live
   *with* the valley.

## What to take (principles)

- **The forester is our answer to "trees run out":** a job that replants. Our Phase 3.5 tree
  plots are exactly this. Banished shows it's a satisfying loop, not a boring one.
- **Finite deposits push trade and expansion.** Stone and iron run out and then you trade; for
  us, scrap and components run out and then you reclaim or send expeditions.
- **Clothing extends how far people can safely go** in winter. That's a great hook for our
  winter plus expeditions.
- **Tools that wear out** give a small, steady demand that keeps workshops busy. Weapons and
  machine wear could do this.
- **Status icons over heads** as the main readability tool. We have badges; keep them the primary
  signal on touch.
- **Diversify or suffer:** same-crop infestations reward variety without a lecture.

## What to avoid

- **Job assignment by headcount.** It's clean, but it's direct control of labor. We keep
  priorities.
- **The slow opening:** Banished's first hour is quiet. Our Director should hit sooner (it does:
  a walker on day 1, a pack on day 3).
- **Scale growing into faceless numbers.** Banished citizens are anonymous; ours have names.

## How it maps to us

Banished is the proof that **renewal is fun when it's a loop you tend,** not a stockpile you
drain. It directly answers the owner's playtest problem: a forester-style job plus finite
deposits that push you outward is the shape of our Phase 3.5 resource loop.
