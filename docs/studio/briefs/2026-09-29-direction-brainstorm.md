# The missing half: what all this building is for

**Status:** brainstorm (step 1 of the vision one-pager) · **Asked by:** owner, 2026-09-29 ·
**Recommendation:** the owner's idea, pushed further: **The Supply Line** (direction D)

## 0. The question

The owner's words:
- *"is the game about the people or the base... thats what i feel most torn about."*
- *"50% of this game overall i dunno what the core game is overall or what is fun or anything
  just buiding and what we see now is kind of my 50%"*

**The known half is building:** the colony, walls, machines, and people you can see today. **The
missing half is the core fun that the building serves.** Every game on the owner's list has one:
- **Cosmoteer:** you design a ship, and a battle tests it.
- **They Are Billions:** you wall a town, and the swarm tests it.
- **Factorio:** you build a factory, and the next bottleneck and the biters test it.
- **RimWorld:** you build a colony, and the storyteller breaks it into stories.

Our building has no test that's fun yet. Hordes arrive, but you can't see why the line failed.
You can't do anything once the fight starts, because the supply chain stops. And nothing pays
out when you win.

There's a second knot underneath. The build has a people sim (needs, mood, bites, the dead rising)
and a fortress sim (fences, turrets, machines, the Din), and neither *needs* the other. That's why
"people or base?" feels torn. **The missing half should be the test the building is for, and it
should tie the two sims together.**

**This direction is wrong if** in the first playable slice a horde still feels like something you
wait out, and the survivors are still background while the player watches only the fence.

---

## 1. Five candidates for the missing half (from all-people to all-fortress)

Each one answers the same question: **what is the building for?**

**A. The Last Family** (all people) · **You build a home.**
- **Fantasy:** keep a handful of people together, and sane, through the end of the world.
- **Loop:** take someone in → learn who they are → keep them fed, housed, and bonded → lose
  someone → the colony changes around the gap.
- **Minute to minute you care about:** who's hurt, who's cracking, and who trusts whom.
- **Retold:** "Mara wouldn't let us burn her brother. She sat with him in the shed until he rose."
- **DNA:** Dwarf Fortress (named things and memory; the collapse is the story), Norland (groups
  that want different things), RimWorld (characters).
- **Biggest risk:** with indirect control you *watch* the drama more than you shape it. It also
  needs a lot of written event content, and the Phase 3 machines become set dressing.
- **Keeps of today's build:** about 60%.

**B. The Walking Colony** (contrarian: you don't hold out, you move on) · **You build to leave.**
- **Fantasy:** stay one step ahead of the tide.
- **Loop:** settle a site → strip it → the Din builds and the dead gather → pack up and leave
  before the tide → the next site.
- **Minute to minute you care about:** how long you can stay, and what's worth carrying.
- **Retold:** "We left the gas station an hour before they hit. From the ridge we watched them
  fill the yard we'd built."
- **DNA:** Factorio, Zomboid, and Banished (depletion pushes you outward), taken to its end.
- **Biggest risk:** it throws away "build *your* compound", the attachment to a place that every
  game on the owner's list has. It also needs travel systems (L+).
- **Keeps:** about 50%.

**C. The Hush** (contrarian: winning is not being heard) · **You build to hide.**
- **Fantasy:** survive by being quiet enough that the tide walks past.
- **Loop:** work loud by day while the hordes are far → go dark at dusk → hold still as a horde
  migrates past → count the cost of every noise.
- **Minute to minute you care about:** the Din meter, the lights, and who's making noise.
- **Retold:** "Eighty of them walked past the fence at 3am with every light off. Then the
  generator kicked on. I'd left the freezer on *Always*."
- **DNA:** Zomboid (volume as a universal currency), Prison Architect (control has costs: lights
  out means cabin fever), Factorio's pollution flipped (output you can hide).
- **Biggest risk:** waiting isn't fun, and "nothing happened" is hard to feel as a win.
- **Keeps:** about 75%.

**D. The Supply Line** (hybrid; **the owner's idea**, developed in §2) · **You build a machine
that people run under fire, and every night tests it.**
- **Fantasy:** your fortress runs on people. Every bullet a turret fires, someone carried there.
- **Loop:** the horde hits the line → magazines drain and fences buckle → runners carry ammo,
  fuel, and scrap under fire → the dead drop what they carried → the harvest feeds the press and
  the vat → a stronger line → louder → a bigger horde.
- **Minute to minute you care about:** which gun is going dry, and whether the runner makes it.
- **Retold:** see §2.
- **DNA:** Cosmoteer (crew carry power; distance is a design decision; fix it with layout),
  Factorio (turrets need feeding), Zomboid (the dead keep what they carried), They Are Billions
  (walls, chokepoints, and clearing that pays twice).
- **Biggest risk:** people become delivery drones, and a battle is hard to read on an iPad.
- **Keeps:** about 90%. Turret magazines, haulers who restock them, the ammo press, the render
  vat, and generator fuel already exist.

**E. The Holdout** (all fortress) · **You build to withstand a siege.**
- **Fantasy:** hold the wall against a siege you can see coming.
- **Loop:** expand → wall off → a swarm is announced with a direction and a countdown → prepare
  → survive → expand past the wall.
- **Minute to minute you care about:** the weakest fence section, and the countdown.
- **Retold:** "Night 9 they came from the north where the wire was thinnest. The second line held
  with four rounds in the last turret."
- **DNA:** They Are Billions (scheduled dread, layered walls), Factorio (evolution).
- **Biggest risk:** a pure defense game with no unit control is They Are Billions with one hand
  tied behind its back. People become units, and we lose the story generator.
- **Keeps:** about 70%.

Any of these can carry the approved arc: reclaim the town district by district, then the
radio-tower siege.

## 2. The owner's idea, taken seriously

*"A defense type game like cosmoteer but zombie attacks. People carrying things to resupply...
zombies dropping items and the colony recycling the zombies."*

### Why it's the right instinct
**It gives the building a test.** This is the loop that makes Cosmoteer fun: design, test,
redesign. By day you design the line. At night the dead test it. At dawn you see exactly where it
broke (the dry gun, the long run, the thin wire) and fix it. The building half is the part the
owner already has, and this is the fun it serves.

It also answers the torn question without choosing a side. **The base is the body; the people are
the blood.** Every fortress problem (a dry turret, a buckling fence) gets solved by a *person* going
somewhere dangerous. Every people problem (a bite, a breakdown, a death) shows up as a *fortress*
problem: the east gun goes dry because Gus is in bed with a fever. You play the base, and the
stories are about the people.

### The loop, nested in time
- **In the moment:** a fill ring on a turret drains, a runner picks up a crate from the cache,
  crosses the yard, and the gun starts firing again.
- **Across a day:** build and scavenge by daylight. **At dusk, pick tonight's volume:** go dark,
  or ring the dinner bell. **Night** is the horde. **At dawn** the gate opens, and the crew goes
  out to strip the killing field before the stragglers come.
- **Across a season:** winter slows the dead but burns fuel, and supply lanes and caches get
  redesigned as the line grows.
- **Across a run:** the line pushes outward into reclaimed districts. The runs get longer, the
  colony gets louder, and it ends at the radio-tower siege.

**Economy:**
- **Sources:** the dead (scrap, ammo, cloth, sometimes medicine or components), corpses (fuel),
  the finite ruins, and tree plots.
- **Converters:** the ammo press (scrap → ammo), the render vat (corpse → biofuel), and the
  generator (biofuel → power).
- **Sinks:** rounds fired, fuel burned, fence and turret repairs, and people.
- **Reinforcing loop:** kills → loot → more guns → more kills.
- **Brakes:** the Din (louder brings bigger hordes, and the noise-drawn dead wreck machines), the
  crowd rule (enough of them push a fence over), and the runner's exposure (bites).

**Paper math from today's numbers** (computed, not playtested):
- **Ammo per kill:** a zombie has 45 HP, and a turret hits for 18–26 at about 60–70% accuracy.
  That's roughly **3 rounds per kill**, so a 60-round magazine is about 20 kills.
- **Break-even on ammo:** the press makes 15 rounds from 5 scrap, so **one kill costs about 1
  scrap**. A zombie that drops about 1 scrap on average pays for its own bullet.
- **Fuel:** a corpse renders into 8 biofuel, and the generator burns 30 a day. So **about 4 kills
  a day runs the lights.** The loop closes at a tiny scale, which is good.

### Where Cosmoteer's "visible logistics, fixed by layout" fits indirect control
It fits *better* here than in Cosmoteer, which already leaves its crew mostly on their own. We'd
give the player three levers, and none of them is an order:
1. **Layout:** where the cache, the press, the vat, and the gate sit, and whether the lane between
   them is covered. A short, walled lane is the fix, just like moving a reactor next to the guns.
2. **Policy:** a third **Response** (Fight / **Supply** / Flee). When the dead come, Supply
   survivors keep the guns fed and the fences patched instead of fighting or hiding. It's a
   column in the Work tab, not a command.
3. **Seeing it:** today you can't see what anyone is carrying, or how full a turret is, without
   tapping it. Cosmoteer is fun because you *watch* the battery cross the ship.

**What the build does today (read from the code, plus one rough test):** when a horde comes, the
alarm sends fighters to the towers and everyone else to shelter. Anyone who sees a zombie drops
their job to fight or flee. **So the supply chain stops exactly when the guns need it.**

The rough test (seed 11, day 1): a fence ring with a gate, two powered turrets, 150 spare rounds
inside, and 25 hunting zombies. The turrets fired 79 rounds and one ran dry. The gate broke,
nobody carried a single round to the guns, and all three survivors were dead within three hours.
The setup was crude, so this proves the gap exists, not the balance.

### What makes it ours, not They Are Billions or Cosmoteer with zombies
- **You set the size of the attack.** The horde is your own noise coming back, and the Din is a
  dial you turn at dusk. Neither of those games lets the player pick tonight's threat.
- **The enemy is the mine.** A fight pays out; it isn't just a cost.
- **A bite on the run is a two-day story.** Cosmoteer's crew die instantly. Ours come back
  bitten, and the colony watches an infection bar while the gun they saved keeps everyone alive.
- **Your own dead are in the horde.** "Zeke (turned)" already walks with his name and clothes.
  Kill him, and what he drops is *his* pistol.

### The retold moment
"At 2am the east turret clicked empty with twenty of them on the wire. Gus, our only runner, took
a crate from the cache and crossed the open yard. The gun started barking again and I actually
cheered. On the way back one squeezed through the gate and bit him. We spent two days watching
his infection bar while that turret held the east side. He pulled through. He never ran again,
and I didn't ask him to." (That's a loss, a recovery, and a person changed by it.)

### Where I'd push it further than the owner said
1. **Add Supply as a third Response.** It's the missing verb, and it stays indirect.
2. **Show every load.** Draw what each survivor carries (an ammo box, a log, a fuel can, a body
   over the shoulder), and put a fill ring on every turret and generator.
3. **Small magazines, placed caches.** A turret holds about one burst (24 rounds is my starting
   point), so it *must* be fed mid-horde. A cache you paint near the line shortens the run.
4. **Drops land where the dead fall,** outside your wire. Collecting the harvest means opening
   the gate at dawn, so greed and safety are one gate.
5. **Runners are people.** Scared runners freeze. Brave ones earn a thought ("Kept the guns
   fed"). The runner you pick matters: your only doctor, or your best shot?

### Where I'd push back
1. **Not tower-defense waves.** Keep the attacks driven by the Director and the Din. Warnings
   with a direction and a countdown are good; fixed waves with upgrade shops would kill the dial.
2. **No crew assigned to specific guns,** and no belts. Cosmoteer lets you post crew at stations;
   that's direct control. Belts are already ruled out by a settled owner decision.
3. **Drops mustn't be piñatas.** If every kill pays well, maximum noise every night is the
   dominant strategy. Keep drops modest and physical, out beyond the wire, and let the Din punish
   greed with bigger hordes.

### The smallest playable slice (about one session, on top of today's build)
1. A **Supply** option in the Response column. During an alarm or a sighting, Supply survivors
   keep doing rearm, refuel, and repair jobs, and only flee from a zombie within about 3 cells.
2. **Draw carried loads, plus fill rings** on turrets and generators.
3. **A turret magazine knob:** 60 → 24, refilling at half.

Zombie drops come next, as the Phase 3.5 harvest. **Test:** the same fence-ring setup on seed 11
with one Supply survivor, compared with none. It works if the runner makes at least 2 mid-fight
refills, the turrets fire at least twice their magazine per horde, and colonies with a runner
last clearly longer. **It's wrong** if the runner almost always dies on the first trip, or never
needs to go.

## 3. Recommendation

**Go with D, the owner's idea, pushed as described in §2.** Four reasons:
- It supplies the missing half: a nightly test that the building exists to pass, in the same
  shape as the owner's favorite game (Cosmoteer).
- It dissolves the people-or-base question instead of forcing a choice.
- It turns Phase 3 and the approved harvest into one loop.
- It keeps about 90% of the build, and its first slice is a single session.

**Runner-up: A (The Last Family).** Take this path if a playtest shows the fortress bores the
owner. Two ideas from other directions fold straight into D anyway:
- **From A:** every survivor arrives tied to someone, so the runner has a sister on the wall.
- **From C:** going dark is a real choice on nights when you can't afford a harvest.

**What this would mean for the one-pager (draft):**
- **Hook:** "Build a fortress that runs on people: someone has to carry every bullet, and the
  dead you kill pay for the next ones."
- **Pillars:**
  1. **The base runs on people:** fixed by layout and policy, never by orders.
  2. **Noise is the dial:** louder is richer and deadlier.
  3. **The dead are the tide and the harvest,** and your own come back.
- **Not:** a tower defense, a factory, or a drama you only watch.

### Questions that would lock it (with what I'll assume)
1. **During a horde, do you want to watch your plan hold or scramble to fix it?** I assume
   mostly watch, with a few live levers: the alarm, Response, machine modes, the cache, and pause.
2. **How big is a colony at its peak?** I assume 5–12 people, few enough that every runner has a
   name you know.
3. **Do you *invite* the dead (ring the dinner bell for the harvest) or only survive them?** I
   assume you invite them. Turning the Din up on purpose is the core gamble.
